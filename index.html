This usually works well, but there is still a risk that the model may answer based on other information from its training data (sometimes OK) or hallucinate entirely incorrect details (definitely bad). The best way to help mitigate these risks is to support the answer with citations that incorporate direct quotations from the underlying source documents. This even acts as a form of fact-checking: the user can confirm that the quoted text did indeed come from those documents, helping provide relatively robust protection against hallucinated details resulting in incorrect answers. Actually building a system that does this can be quite tricky. Matt Yeung described a pattern for this he called Deterministic Quoting last April, where answers are accompanied by direct quotations from the source documents that are guaranteed to be copied across and not lossily transformed by the model.
